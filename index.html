    Models used (summary)

    The full derivation, parameters and assumptions are in TECHNICAL_REPORT.md. All models are simplified teaching models. None of them is a validated physiological or device model.

    ECG: McSharry dynamical model

    A point moves on a limit cycle in the (x, y) plane with angular speed ω = 2π/RRi. Its height z is pushed by five Gaussian events, P, Q, R, S and T:

    ż = − Σi∈{P,Q,R,S,T} ai Δθi exp(−Δθi² / 2bi²) − (z − z0), Δθi = (θ − θi) mod 2π

    Each beat interval RRi is 60/HR plus Gaussian jitter (SD set by the variability control). Irregular mode draws it uniformly within ±35 %. Noise, baseline wander and mains interference are added. Then the display filter (band-pass, notch) is applied. A simplified Pan–Tompkins detector (derivative, square, moving-window integral, adaptive threshold) finds the R peaks for the HR estimate. The ECG is never generated from a measured heart rate.

    Pulse oximetry: ratio of ratios

    Iλ(t) = I0 exp(−kλ · PI · s(t)) → R = (ACred/DCred) / (ACIR/DCIR) → SpO₂ ≈ 110 − 25 R

    The linear calibration line is a textbook approximation. Real oximeters use empirical calibration curves. The signal quality index (SQI) measures how consistent R stays over 4 one-second windows. Motion adds a common-mode artifact that pulls R toward 1 (SpO₂ ≈ 85 %).

    NIBP: oscillometric method

    The cuff inflates above the expected systolic pressure, then deflates linearly. The oscillation amplitude of each beat follows the slope of an arctangent pressure–volume curve of the artery at transmural pressure Part − Pcuff. MAPosc is the cuff pressure at the maximum amplitude Am. SBP is where the amplitude falls to Ks·Am above the maximum. DBP is where it falls to Kd·Am below the maximum.

    MAPformula = (SBP + 2·DBP) / 3 PP = SBP − DBP (derived values, not measured)

    Respiration: impedance pneumography

    The transthoracic impedance changes with lung volume. A phase oscillator at RR/60 Hz drives the breath waveform, and a cardiogenic component and noise are added. A band-pass filter and hysteresis threshold detect breaths. If no breath is detected for the apnea delay, apnea is declared.

    Temperature: first-order probe

    dTprobe/dt = (Ttrue − Tprobe) / τ

    Capnography: time-based CO₂ waveform

    The same breath oscillator drives the capnogram. During inspiration CO₂ falls to the inspired level FiCO₂ (phase 0). During expiration it stays at FiCO₂ for the dead space (phase I), rises with time constant τ (phase II) and ends on a slightly rising plateau (phase III). EtCO₂ is the maximum of each breath.

    c(v) = FiCO₂ + (Pplateau − FiCO₂) · (1 − e−v/τ) / (1 − e−1/τ), τ = 0.035 + 0.35 · obstruction

    Pplateau falls with cardiac output: with no perfusion (V-Fib, asystole) little CO₂ reaches the lungs, so EtCO₂ drops. Airway obstruction slows phase II ("shark fin"). Rebreathing lifts the inspired baseline. These are qualitative teaching shapes. Gas sampling delay and water traps are not modelled.

    Invasive arterial pressure: second-order transducer

    The fluid-filled catheter and transducer act as a second-order system with natural frequency fn and damping ratio ζ (Gardner 1981):

    ẍ = ωn² (Part − h·0.7356 − x) − 2ζωn ẋ, ωn = 2π fn

    Optimal: fn 20 Hz, ζ 0.6. Under-damped: 6 Hz, ζ 0.1 (systolic overshoot, ringing). Over-damped: 4 Hz, ζ 1.5 (blunted systolic, raised diastolic). The mean pressure is almost unchanged in each case. h is the transducer height above the heart in cm; 1 cm of water ≈ 0.7356 mmHg. A line open to air reads 0.

    Rhythm analysis: rule-based teaching analyser

    The monitor band-passes the acquired ECG (0.5–40 Hz) and measures each detected QRS width. It then applies simple rules every second: almost flat trace → asystole; no QRS for 4 s on a busy trace, or wide chaotic complexes → V-Fib; the last 4 complexes wide above 100 bpm → V-Tach; the last 8 narrow, very regular and above 150 bpm → SVT (P waves are not checked, so a fast sinus rhythm gets the same label); narrow-to-narrow RR coefficient of variation above 0.12 → irregular; any wide complex in the last 60 s → PVCs. The first 8 s are "learning". The thresholds were tuned on this simulator's own waveforms. This is not a validated arrhythmia algorithm (IEC 60601-2-27 requires testing against annotated databases) and it gives no diagnosis.

    Main components of a bedside monitor

    This is a generic block diagram from the textbook literature (Webster, Medical Instrumentation; Webster, Design of Pulse Oximeters; Allen 2007; Forouzanfar et al. 2015). It describes no specific product and claims no device specifications.

    • ECG channel: skin electrodes and lead wires → protected, isolated input → instrumentation (differential) amplifier with high common-mode rejection → band-pass and optional mains notch filter → analogue-to-digital converter (ADC) → QRS detector → heart rate.
    • Impedance respiration: a small high-frequency carrier current passes through the ECG electrodes. The impedance change caused by breathing is demodulated, filtered and counted as breaths.
    • SpO₂ channel: a probe with a red LED, an infrared LED and one photodiode → transimpedance amplifier → red and IR signals separated by time multiplexing → AC/DC extraction → ratio of ratios R → calibration curve → SpO₂ and pulse rate.
    • NIBP channel: cuff, air pump, deflation valve and pressure transducer → the controller inflates, then deflates → the oscillations on the cuff pressure are extracted → envelope → MAP, then SBP and DBP by ratio rules. An over-pressure limit stops inflation.
    • Temperature channel: thermistor probe in a bridge or divider → ADC → linearisation to °C. The probe responds with a time lag.
    • Processing and output: processor; display of waveforms and numerics; alarm system with limits, priorities, delays and audio/visual signals (IEC 60601-1-8); trend storage; network or serial interface to a central station.

    In this app each channel is a module in src/sim/. Each sensor control (noise, mains, contact, motion, probe state, time constant) acts on the stage of the chain that it names.

    Simplifying assumptions

    • One ECG lead with a fixed morphology. The arrhythmias are six teaching patterns (AF, SVT, PVCs, V-Tach, V-Fib, asystole). Blocks, flutter and paced rhythms are not modelled.
    • Perfusion is one number between 0 and 1. It falls when no effective beat occurs and drives the arterial pressure, the pulse and the EtCO₂.
    • The pulse has a fixed shape and arrives 0.20 s after the R wave. ECG, PPG, pressure and respiration share the same beat times.
    • The PPG uses two wavelengths and a linear calibration. Venous pulsation, ambient light and dyshaemoglobins (COHb, MetHb) are not modelled.
    • Breathing is regular, with I:E = 1:2.
    • NIBP uses an arctangent artery model, linear deflation and fixed ratios. The cuff and limb tissue are not modelled.
    • Parameters are independent: lowering SpO₂ does not raise HR. Alarm limits and delays are illustrative defaults.

    Engineering limitations

    • Motion: motion pulls R toward 1, so the displayed SpO₂ drifts toward about 85 % while the SQI falls. At high motion the NIBP measurement fails with a motion message.
    • Fixed-ratio NIBP: SBP and DBP are estimates, not measurements. In the hypotension preset the DBP estimate can stay just above the alarm limit.
    • Detector: the QRS detector has no search-back. Dropouts and heavy noise can cause missed beats and a wrong HR.
    • Not represented:
      • oximeter bias from skin pigmentation and low perfusion (FDA 2021)
      • cuff-size errors
      • validated arrhythmia detection (the analyser here is illustrative)
      • pacemaker pulses
      • catheter whip, air bubbles and clots in the arterial line
      • sidestream gas-sampling delay
      • electrosurgical interference
      • electrical safety and isolation
